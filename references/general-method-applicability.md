# General Method Applicability

Read this reference for every manuscript before any method-specific reference. Use it when the method is unfamiliar or absent from the specialized files. The purpose is to evaluate the logic of inquiry without relying on a finite catalog of methods.

## Question and inferential goal

- Identify whether the study is descriptive, explanatory, causal, predictive, prescriptive, normative, exploratory, or confirmatory.
- Check whether the selected method can support that goal.
- Check alignment among the unit of analysis, decision unit, observation unit, time scale, and claimed level of inference.
- Identify the counterfactual, comparison, benchmark, or reference state implicit in the question.

## Constructs and representation

- Check whether constructs, variables, agents, states, actions, outcomes, and constraints are defined precisely.
- Check whether the operational representation preserves the meaning of the theoretical concept.
- Look for construct overlap, proxy contamination, label substitution, and outcomes embedded in predictors.
- Check whether aggregation or discretization removes variation needed for the claim.
- Require broad umbrella constructs such as digital transformation, intelligence, resilience, sustainability, or platform capability to be decomposed into observable capabilities, interventions, or decisions that can affect the stated operational problem.

## Management specificity and practical chain

- Trace the proposed chain from technology, strategy, policy, or construct to an identifiable actor, decision, implementation mechanism, operational constraint, and outcome.
- Check whether the focal solution can actually act on the operational problem. A broad digital or organizational label does not establish that a proposed intervention changes capacity, information, incentives, coordination, lead time, quality, cost, risk, or another relevant mechanism.
- Apply a technology substitution test. Replace the focal technology with a plausible alternative and determine which assumptions, decisions, mechanisms, or predictions should change.
- Apply a variable substitution test. Replace each focal explanatory variable with a nearby construct or alternative solution and determine whether the argument and result remain unchanged.
- When substitution leaves the theory, model, empirical specification, and implications intact, require the authors to establish topic-specific content validity and a unique explanatory mechanism.
- Distinguish a statistically distinct coefficient or model term from a substantively distinct management explanation.
- Check whether recommendations identify who should act, what action should change, under which conditions, through which mechanism, and with what operational tradeoff.

## Narrative and reality anchoring

- Reconstruct the research story as a sequence connecting the practical problem, theoretical tension, focal mechanism, research design, result, and implication.
- Check whether section transitions advance this story rather than present disconnected constructs, technologies, methods, and results.
- Look for a real case, industry episode, institutional fact, operational example, or decision scenario that makes the mechanism and boundary conditions understandable.
- Use cases and examples to clarify plausibility and relevance. Do not treat an illustrative example as validation or causal evidence unless it is sampled and analyzed as part of the research design.
- Require examples to be sufficiently specific to reveal actors, decisions, constraints, and outcomes rather than serving as promotional anecdotes.

## Mechanism and assumptions

- Map each substantive mechanism to an observable, equation, rule, transition, intervention, or comparison in the design.
- List the assumptions needed for identification, estimation, solution, prediction, or interpretation.
- Distinguish assumptions chosen for tractability from claims about reality.
- Check whether assumptions are mutually consistent and plausible in the stated setting.
- Examine limiting cases and conditions under which the mechanism disappears or reverses.

## Evidence and identification

- Determine what in the data, model, cases, or design identifies the focal relationship.
- Check whether alternative explanations can generate the same result.
- Check selection, simultaneity, reverse causality, measurement error, omitted structure, and dependence as applicable.
- Determine whether the evidence separates the focal mechanism from a simpler baseline.
- Require causal language to match the identification strategy.

## Procedure and reproducibility

- Check whether an independent researcher could reconstruct the data, preprocessing, model, analysis, solution, or synthesis.
- Require transparent inputs, transformations, exclusions, parameter choices, software versions, algorithms, and stopping rules as applicable.
- Check whether discretionary choices are justified before or independently of the desired result.
- Check whether code, data, equations, derivations, prompts, seeds, and supplementary materials are sufficient or whether limitations are disclosed.

## Estimation, solution, or computation

- Check mathematical, statistical, algorithmic, and logical correctness at the level supported by the manuscript.
- Check existence, uniqueness, identifiability, convergence, feasibility, and boundary behavior when relevant.
- Check whether approximation or numerical error is controlled.
- Compare the chosen procedure with credible alternatives and baselines.
- Check whether hyperparameters or parameter ranges are justified and fully reported.

## Validation

- Identify what would count as evidence that the method is wrong or inadequate.
- Check internal validity, external validity, predictive validity, construct validity, face validity, and reality consistency as applicable.
- Look for out-of-sample, holdout, replication, alternative-measure, alternative-model, cross-context, or case validation.
- Check whether validation data are independent of model construction and tuning.
- Do not accept a robustness exercise that preserves the disputed assumption and merely changes notation or one parameter.

## Uncertainty and sensitivity

- Check sampling, parameter, model, measurement, scenario, and numerical uncertainty as relevant.
- Identify assumptions or inputs with the greatest influence on the result.
- Require sensitivity analysis over defensible ranges rather than selected favorable values.
- Check discontinuities, thresholds, multiple equilibria, nonlinearity, and unstable rankings.
- Check whether uncertainty changes the substantive conclusion.

## Interpretation and scope

- Distinguish mathematical implication, empirical association, prediction, causal effect, and normative recommendation.
- Check whether results are nontrivial or direct restatements of assumptions.
- Apply the substitution test. Replace the focal technology, industry, or sustainability label with a generic alternative and assess whether the theory and result remain unchanged.
- Check whether generalization is supported across populations, periods, institutions, markets, or parameter regions.
- Bound managerial and policy implications by the actors, decisions, mechanisms, and interventions actually modeled or observed.
- For a Management Insights or equivalent section, require an explicit translation from findings to real decisions, implementation conditions, likely tradeoffs, and limits. Generic calls to invest, adopt, collaborate, or improve awareness are insufficient.

## Visual evidence

- Check whether every figure is legible at normal reading size and defines axes, units, legends, symbols, samples, scenarios, and uncertainty where relevant.
- Check whether all comparisons needed for the claim are shown on compatible scales and with a complete set of relevant cases, baselines, parameter regions, or groups.
- Look for cropped ranges, inconsistent scales, hidden overlap, color-only distinctions, selective panels, or missing uncertainty that can distort comparison.
- Cross-check figures against text, tables, equations, and supplementary results.

## Method-independent warning signs

- The research question is broader than the analysis.
- The core result is encoded in an assumption or definition.
- The method cannot distinguish the focal mechanism from a baseline explanation.
- Key choices are justified only after results are shown.
- The analysis reports precision without addressing structural uncertainty.
- Validation reuses the same data or assumptions that created the result.
- Robustness checks are numerous but do not target the main threat.
- The conclusion changes the inferential goal after seeing the results.
- A broad management or technology label is not connected to a concrete operational decision or mechanism.
- Focal variables can be exchanged for nearby constructs without changing the explanation.
- The managerial implications repeat fashionable recommendations without specifying actors, actions, feasibility, or tradeoffs.

Treat these as prompts for investigation, not automatic reasons for rejection.

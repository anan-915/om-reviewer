# Decision Calibration

Recommendations are advice to the editor. The editor makes the final decision. Base severity on the consequence of a problem and the scale of repair, not on tone, paper length, or the number of comments.

The number of comments does not determine the recommendation. Reject and Major Revision reports normally contain eight to ten distinct high-value concerns. Minor Revision reports normally contain three to six. Use fewer or more when the evidence requires it. A Major Revision may exceed ten concerns when the core study remains viable and the additional problems are distinct and repairable.

## Fatal flaw

A flaw is fatal when the central claims cannot be supported without replacing the study's evidentiary foundation. Typical cases include:

- No distinguishable contribution after comparison with the relevant literature
- A research question that the design or model cannot answer
- Construct or outcome contamination that invalidates the central test
- Identification failure requiring new data or a fundamentally different design
- A model whose main result is an artifact of indefensible assumptions or timing
- Missing derivation, feasibility, or equilibrium conditions that undermine the whole analytical result
- Irrecoverable mismatch between data, analysis, and principal conclusions
- Integrity or data-access problems that prevent reliable evaluation

One fatal flaw may be sufficient for Reject. Explain why ordinary revision cannot repair it.

## Major correctable problem

A problem is major when it threatens an important claim but can plausibly be repaired within the existing study. Examples include:

- Incomplete theory or unclear boundary conditions
- A fixable specification or validation weakness
- Missing robustness that directly targets a credible threat
- Underdeveloped synthesis in a review article
- Missing parameter conditions or sensitivity analysis when the model foundation remains viable
- Conclusions and implications that require substantial narrowing

Use Major Revision when the contribution remains plausible and the required work is concrete and feasible.

## Minor problem

A problem is minor when it does not threaten the central contribution or validity. Examples include:

- Local clarification of definitions or procedures
- Bounded reporting corrections
- Figure, table, equation, citation, or section-number fixes
- Limited language editing
- Narrow qualification of an implication

Use Minor Revision only when no unresolved fatal or major problem remains.

## Accept

Use Accept rarely. The paper should be methodologically sound, clearly reported, appropriately bounded, and suitable for the target journal without substantive revision.

## Decision process

1. Identify the most consequential issue.
2. Ask whether the central contribution survives if that issue is corrected.
3. Estimate whether correction requires clarification, reanalysis, model extension, redesign, or new evidence.
4. Check whether multiple major problems interact to make repair unrealistic.
5. Compare the recommendation with the wording of comments to authors.
6. State confidence as high, medium, or low based on completeness of the files and verification.

## User-requested recommendation

- Record `recommendation_source` as `user` when the user specifies the outcome.
- Use the requested outcome to frame the report, but do not invent concerns, evidence, or certainty.
- If the evidence materially conflicts with the request, state the mismatch in the Chinese assessment and confidential editor note.
- Keep comments to authors factual and constructive regardless of the requested outcome.

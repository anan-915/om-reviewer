# Revision Review

Use this reference for R1, R2, or later rounds and whenever prior reviews or an author response are supplied.

## Build the response matrix

For every prior concern, record these elements privately.

- The original request
- The author's stated response
- The actual manuscript change and verified location
- Any new analysis, evidence, or citation
- Status as resolved, partially resolved, unresolved, not applicable, or newly introduced problem

Do not treat a persuasive response letter as evidence that the manuscript changed. Check the revised file.

## Evaluate each response

- Determine whether the response addresses the scientific substance rather than only wording.
- Check whether new analysis uses appropriate data, method, assumptions, and reporting.
- Check whether a rejected request is answered with a defensible reason and whether the manuscript now states the resulting limitation.
- Check consistency among the response letter, marked manuscript, clean manuscript, tables, figures, appendices, and supplementary files.
- Verify every claimed page, section, table, figure, and equation location.
- Check whether a local fix creates contradictions elsewhere.

## Calibrate the new report

- Open by acknowledging material improvements that are verifiably present.
- Focus the report on unresolved, partially resolved, and newly introduced high-priority problems.
- Do not repeat resolved comments merely to preserve the length or tone of the first report.
- Do not raise a new major demand that could reasonably have been raised earlier unless the revision created it or the issue is essential to validity.
- Distinguish author disagreement from failure to respond.
- Explain why any remaining issue still affects contribution, validity, interpretation, or presentation.

## Recommendation logic

- Keep or strengthen a reject recommendation when the revision cannot repair a fatal design or contribution problem.
- Recommend another major revision only when the remaining work is substantial but realistically repairable.
- Recommend minor revision when the core evidence is sound and only bounded corrections remain.
- Do not reward the quantity of changes. Judge whether the decisive issues are resolved.
